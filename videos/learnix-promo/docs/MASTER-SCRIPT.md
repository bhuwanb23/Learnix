**A premium enterprise product film · v1.0**

---

## 1. Logline

> A modern college looks calm from outside. Inside, four people are working from four different systems, and the institution's data — abundant everywhere, connected nowhere. One platform collapses that fragmentation into a single core, and the college becomes legible to itself for the first time.

---

## 2. Positioning

> **One institution.**
> **One connected platform.**
> **One source of truth.**

Closing line: **The digital operating system for modern colleges.**

The film must never read as "a college management app." Every beat is framed as **infrastructure** — the thing the institution runs on.

---

## 3. Audience & objectives

**Audience:** principals, directors, registrars, department heads, faculty, students, parents, investors, institutional partners.

**By the end, the viewer must hold all ten of these:**

1. Traditional college management is broken
2. Fragmentation is the root cause, not missing data
3. It hurts students, faculty and management differently
4. Existing tools add another portal instead of removing one
5. Learnix is a connected core, not a module list
6. The institution becomes genuinely connected
7. Each role gets the interface it needs
8. Management gets real-time institutional visibility
9. Students and faculty get their time back
10. This is durable central infrastructure

**Governing rule (§29):** *Problem → Human consequence → Solution → Experience → Institutional impact.* No beat may be a feature with no human consequence attached to it.

## 6. Visual language

**Three worlds (§27). Every scene is tagged.**

| World | Meaning | Treatment |
|---|---|---|
| **REAL** | People, campus, corridors, offices | Live action / generated plate. Natural light, shallow DOF, 35mm-equivalent. Handheld in chaos, locked-off in calm. |
| **DIGITAL** | The mobile app and web platform | HyperFrames. Screen-space, perfectly frontal, zero perspective distortion. The interface is always square to camera. |
| **SYSTEM** | Architecture, data, workflows, AI | HyperFrames. Abstract but grounded — never floating holograms. Ink on light, 1px rules, nodes on a visible grid. |

**World-transition grammar — the film's signature move:**

> Real action triggers a Digital surface → camera **pushes through the screen plane** → Digital becomes SYSTEM as data travels → camera **pulls back out** to the Real world, where something has visibly changed.

Used in S37, S43, S46, S51. This is what makes the technology feel like it operates *underneath* the world rather than beside it.

**Wardrobe locks:**
- Act 1 (calm): natural morning light, light casual, neutral palette
- Act 2 (chaos): the same characters, later in day, colder overhead light, tighter framing
- Act 10 (resolution): *identical wardrobe to Act 1*, same locations, same light angle — this is the before/after device

**Colour logic:** the chaos world is desaturated toward cool grey with practical monitor spill. The product world is on-brand — `#F5F7F9` canvas, `#0F172A` ink, `#2563EB` primary, with role accents (library amber `#B45309`, transport blue, success `#059669`). Amber is reserved throughout for **"blocked on someone else"** — it is a semantic colour, never decoration.

**Forbidden:** stock-footage feeling, generic corporate gloss, cartoon characters, holographic UI, purple-blue AI gradients, dashboard-on-a-loop, excessive text, isometric 3D campus.

## 7. Music arc (§24)

Seven movements. The score is a single continuous build that **drops to silence** at S22 and rebuilds from nothing.

| # | Movement | Time | Character | Reference feeling |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| M1 | Morning | 0:00–0:30 | Minimal piano, sparse, warm — one instrument, room air | Anticipation, not commerce |
| M2 | Fracture | 0:30–1:30 | Low pulse enters under S10. Layered percussive rhythm builds. Not aggressive — *accumulating* | Pressure, fragmentation |
| M3 | Collapse | 1:30–1:52 | Everything cuts. **Silence.** Sub-bass only | Disorientation, weight |
| M4 | Turn | 2:00–2:20 | One sustained tone, then a single piano note answered by a synth pad | Relief, first breath |
| M5 | Architecture | 2:20–3:30 | Modern, precise, mid-tempo electronic. Arpeggiated but not busy | Confidence, capability |
| M6 | Human | 3:30–5:00 | Warmth enters. Softer than M5. The score becomes *about people* again | Relief, ease |
| M7 | Intelligence | 5:00–6:15 | Sophisticated, forward-leaning, restrained | Foresight, control |
| M8 | Resolve | 6:15–7:00 | Full, emotional, cinematic. The M1 piano returns, now answered by strings | Optimism, arrival |

**Rules:** no corporate-stock "inspiring" swell; no beat drop; no lyric or vocal sample; the M3 silence at S22 is non-negotiable and must be *felt*.

**ACTION:** Slow crane drift. One early student crosses the frame at the bottom, small in the architecture.

**DIALOGUE:** —
**VOICE-OVER:** *A college is a large machine that most people only see from the outside.*
**ON-SCREEN TEXT:** —
**UI:** —
**DIAGRAM:** —

**CAMERA:** Aerial establishing → slow descending crane. 35mm. Wide, locked horizon.

**TRANSITION:** Hard cut on the crane's last frame.

### S10 · 0:40–0:47 · 7s
**WORLD:** REAL
**LOCATION:** Faculty office / cabin
**CHARACTERS:** PROF. SHARMA
**PURPOSE:** The faculty fragmentation scene. The longest in Act 2, and the thesis of Act 2.

**VISUAL:** Anita at a desk. Window light. A laptop, a printed register, a phone.

**ACTION:** She marks attendance on a **spreadsheet**. Types names. A student name is misspelled in the sheet — she corrects it. She switches to the college portal to upload, finds a different roll-number format, maps it by hand. Picks up the phone to message a colleague about the format. Reaches for the printed register to check one number. The loop begins again for the next section.

**DIALOGUE:** PROF. SHARMA (into phone): "What's the format for this section? …Roll or reg number? …Okay. Reg. Sorry."
**VOICE-OVER:** *A register, a portal, a spreadsheet and a phone call — to record who was present.*
**ON-SCREEN TEXT:** —
**UI:** Spreadsheet window and portal window, visually unrelated. Roll-number mismatch visible.

**DIAGRAM:** —

**CAMERA:** Locked-off medium that slowly widens — the desk revealing more surfaces than one person can hold. Minimal camera movement; the *desk* does the acting.

**TRANSITION:** Time-lapse jump within the same framing, then cut.

**MUSIC:** M2 builds — third rhythmic layer.

**SOUND DESIGN:** Keyboard, mouse, phone dialling, chair. Each system gets its own distinct sound signature.

**EMOTIONAL INTENT:** This is the film's most important problem beat. Not incompetence — a competent person losing her afternoon to reconciliation. The audience should feel the loss of time, not the loss of competence.

# ACT 3 — THE REAL PROBLEM
### 1:30–2:00 · 5 scenes

---

### S18 · 1:30–1:36 · 6s
**WORLD:** SYSTEM
**LOCATION:** Abstract dark
**CHARACTERS:** —
**PURPOSE:** Restate the problem in a single, calm image after the noise.

**VISUAL:** The frozen grid resolves, slowly, into a clean **network diagram** — clean lines, clear nodes, beautiful. And the lines are broken everywhere.

**Act 6–8 sound signatures — one timbre only.** A single notification sound for the whole system. The *audible* proof that these have become one thing. In S41, its absence does the work.

**Never:** a swoosh on every transition; a bass hit on every reveal; a "whoosh" under any type.

## 13. Appendix C — Hybrid production pipeline

You chose **B**. Concretely, this means three production streams and one composite.

**Stream 1 — Live/generated plates (REAL world)**
Scenes S01–S07, S08–S15, S36–S37 (real portions), S40–S41, S54–S58, S59.
Four actors, two locations, one shoot day minimum. Deliverables: 4K, 24fps, **log or flat** (not baked).

**Stream 2 — HyperFrames compositions (DIGITAL + SYSTEM worlds)**
All interface scenes and all fourteen diagrams. Canvas 1920×1080 @ 30fps.

> **Technical requirement before Phase 8:** HyperFrames currently paints the canvas colour onto the index `#root`, which is correct for a self-contained film but **opaque**. For compositing, each REAL↔DIGITAL transition shot must render with **alpha** (`ProRes 4444` or PNG sequence with transparency). This is a pipeline task to confirm with the CLI before production — it affects the push-through transitions in S36, S37, S42 and S50, which are the film's signature move. **Flagging now because it is cheap to solve early and expensive to solve late.**

**Stream 3 — Composite (NLE)**
Resolve or After Effects. Plate + HyperFrames layer, unified grade and grain across all three streams, so the digital world feels photographed rather than pasted.

**The critical craft rule:** the three worlds must share one grade and one grain. If the digital world is cleaner than the live action, the film reads as a slideshow with footage.

**Sequence:** Build Stream 2 first (it is deterministic, seek-safe and reviewable), then shoot to it. Do not shoot and hope the interface matches.
