# Music Brief — Learnix Master Film

Eight movements, 7:00. Single continuous composition, not eight cues. It rises, breaks, goes silent, and rebuilds.

**Source:** `MASTER-SCRIPT.md` §7. This brief adds what a composer or generator needs and the screenplay does not specify: key, tempo map, instrumentation, and per-scene density.

---

## The one instruction

**Do not make this sound like software.**

The failure mode for an ERP film is a cue that sounds like software — corporate piano, inspirational swell, a beat drop at the moment the product appears. Every note here exists to avoid that.

The score should sound like it scored a **place**, not a platform. Reference feeling: institutional, patient, slightly worn. The college is real and the film respects it.

### Hard exclusions

| Excluded | Why |
|---|---|
| Corporate "inspiring" swell | Instantly reads as a vendor video |
| Beat drop of any kind | Wrong genre entirely |
| Lyrics or vocal samples | This is a narrated film; the VO is the only voice |
| Rhythmic perfection / four-on-the-floor | Nobody works to a metronome |
| Anything that resolves too happily before 6:15 | The film's optimism has to be earned |
| **Trailer percussion** — taiko, big hits, risers | The film is 7 minutes of understatement |

---

## Global specification

| | |
|---|---|
| Duration | 7:00 exactly |
| Tempo | **76 BPM** throughout. No tempo changes. Tempo stability is the score's spine. |
| Key | **D minor**, resolving to **D major** only in the last 25 seconds |
| Meter | 4/4. Occasional 7/4 in M2 to feel slightly wrong. |
| Sample | 48kHz / 24-bit |
| Peak | −6 dBFS integrated. The narration sits at −12 LUFS and always wins. |
| Stems | Deliver piano, synth, percussion, sub, strings **separately**, plus a full mix |

The D minor → D major move in the last 25 seconds is the score's argument. Everything before it sits in minor, including the entire problem and the entire solution. The film only becomes major when it stops and holds on the closing card.

## Tempo and key map

| Movement | Time | Key | Role |
|---|---|---|---|
| M1 Morning | 0:00–0:30 | D minor (natural) | Piano alone, establishes the motif |
| M2 Fracture | 0:30–1:30 | D minor, drifting | Pulse and percussion accumulate |
| M3 Collapse | 1:30–1:52 | D minor, no root | Sub-bass only. Root note is absent. |
| — silence — | 1:52–2:00 | — | **Nothing.** See below. |
| M4 Turn | 2:00–2:20 | D minor, root returns | One tone, then one piano note |
| M5 Architecture | 2:20–3:30 | D minor (harmonic) | Full electronic ensemble |
| M6 Human | 3:30–5:00 | D minor → F major | Warmth. Brief major colour, returns to minor |
| M7 Intelligence | 5:00–6:15 | D minor (Dorian) | Forward-leaning, brighter third |
| M8 Resolve | 6:15–7:00 | **D major at 6:35** | Piano motif returns, strings answer |

### The M3 silence

`1:52–2:00` is **eight seconds of absolute silence**, and it is the most important eight seconds in the score.

This is where S22 lands — *"So the work comes back to a person"* — with no music under it. A room-tone bed only. The audience should feel the absence as a physical gap. This is not a fade; the score has already stopped at 1:52 and does not resume until 2:00.

Do not bridge it. Do not put a reverse swell into it. Do not shorten it.

## Per-movement detail

### M1 · Morning `0:00–0:30` · S01–S07

**Piano. One instrument. Room air.**

- Single piano, felt-damped, close-miked. You should hear the room.
- Four-note motif in D minor. Introduced complete at **0:06** (S02).
- Sparse. Roughly one phrase every 6 seconds. This is anticipation, not commerce.
- Enters at 0:00 under S01 with **no fade-in** — it is simply there at frame 1. The film starts already observed.
- Under S06 and S07, thin to a single sustained note and let S07's bridge run over near-silence.
- **No percussion. No pad. Piano alone until 0:30.**

### M2 · Fracture `0:30–1:30` · S08–S17

**Accumulating, not aggressive.** This is the movement most likely to be ruined by over-cooking it.

- **0:30 (S08)** — low pulse enters, felt not heard. Sits under the dialogue.
- **0:40 (S10)** — sub-bass pedal note begins. Sustained. This is the film's floor from here to 1:52.
- Layered percussion builds in **three waves**, never all at once:
  - Wave 1, ~0:55 — muted, wooden. Repeated taps.
  - Wave 2, ~1:10 — a dry rim click, off-grid, slightly human
  - Wave 3, ~1:25 — low brushed texture, swelling
- Occasional **7/4 bars** to make the grid feel unstable.
- The M1 piano motif becomes fragmented — played in pieces, with gaps. It is still the same melody, disassembled.
- **Never a fill. Never a build to a hit.** There is no drop coming.
- Ends by thinning, not stopping.

### M3 · Collapse `1:30–1:52` · S18–S21

**Everything cuts. Silence. Sub-bass only.**

- **1:30 hard stop.** No transition. All percussion, piano and pad cut to nothing on the frame.
- Sub-bass drone only, unpitched enough that it is felt rather than identified.
- Sustained through S18–S21.
- **1:52 — stop completely.** Into the eight-second silence.
- This is the film's one hard musical cut and it must be **felt** as violence. Eight seconds of nothing after ninety seconds of density is the loudest thing in the film.

### — Silence `1:52–2:00` · S22

Room tone only. See above. **Non-negotiable.**

### M4 · Turn `2:00–2:20` · S23–S26

**Relief. The score learns to play again.**

- **2:00** — one sustained tone fades up over ~1.5 seconds. This is the sound of the score restarting.
- **2:05 (S24)** — **one single piano note.** The M1 motif's first note, alone. It is the only piano note in 30 seconds of film and it should feel like recognition.
- A soft synth pad enters beneath, still and wide.
- Do not add rhythm. Not yet. M4 has no pulse — the pulse returns in M5 and the return should feel like relief.
- Ends on a held tone that M5 grows out of.

### M5 · Architecture `2:20–3:30` · S27–S35

**Modern, precise, confident.** The film's thesis statement.

- Arpeggiated electronic enters at **2:20**. Steady, mid-tempo, unhurried. This is not a club track.
- The M1 piano motif now runs **underneath the arpeggio** as a sustained bed — the melody is now infrastructure rather than the subject.
- **2:20 (S27)** — the eight-module ignition gets one subtle percussive tick per module. Eight ticks, one per second, matching the diagram exactly. These must land on the animation, not before or after.
- Precision is the point: everything is on the grid here, deliberately, after M2's instability.
- Build across S28–S34 by adding layers, never volume alone.
- **3:11–3:30 (S35)** — the film's consolidation. The arpeggio stops. What remains is a held chord and the piano motif alone. This is the reveal: it was always about the one thing underneath.

### M6 · Human `3:30–5:00` · S36–S45

**Warmth returns. Softer than M5.** The score becomes about people again.

- Percussion thins to almost nothing. This is the quietest the score has been since M1.
- Warm analogue pad replaces the cold arpeggio.
- Brief colour shift toward **F major** around S38 — a moment of daylight, passing through, not arriving.
- **3:30 (S36) is silent.** The phone reveal is silent. Music returns at S37.
- Thins again through Act 7 — S42 and S45 are visual beats and get space.
- This movement must not peak. It exists to make M7 feel like an escalation.

### M7 · Intelligence `5:00–6:15` · S46–S54

**Sophisticated, forward-leaning, restrained.**

- Shift to **Dorian** — brighter third, still minor underneath. The lift is subtle and should not be consciously noticed.
- Add a gentle forward motion, the closest thing the film has to momentum. Still no drums.
- **5:08 (S47)** — one single low notification tone, musically integrated but distinct. It is the only event-sound the score owns.
- **5:48 (S52)** — begin the widening. Add high strings, very quiet, preparing M8.
- **5:40–6:15** — the score opens up spatially. This is the only place in the film where the mix gets wide.
- Does not resolve. It is still reaching when M8 begins.

### M8 · Resolve `6:15–7:00` · S55–S61

**Arrival, not conclusion.** Optimism without triumph.

- **6:15** — the M1 piano motif returns, **complete, in D major for the first time in the film.** Same melody, different key. That is the entire emotional arc of the score in one interval.
- **6:35** — full string section enters underneath, answering the piano. Not swelling — arriving.
- **6:45** — S58, the rhyme frame. Score holds steady. **No swell here.** The calm is the point; the film has already told you it worked.
- **6:52 (S60)** — logo resolve tone. Integrates with the S26 sound-design motif.
- **6:57 (S61)** — final sustain, then **silence from 6:58**. Do not fade out. Stop.
- **No CTA sting. No brand hit.** The film ends the way it began: held, still, observed.

## Under-vo mix rules

The narration carries the film. The score must behave accordingly.

| Situation | Score level |
|---|---|
| Under VO | −18 to −22 LUFS, ducking 2–3 dB |
| Between VO cues (bridges, silent scenes) | up to −9 LUFS |
| **M3 silence 1:52–2:00** | room tone only, −48 LUFS |
| S06, S17, S23, S26, S36, S41, S45, S54, S56, S57 | score may come up — these are visual scenes |
| Final 3 seconds | full, no VO competing |

**Never duck the score more than 3 dB.** Heavy pumping under narration is the fastest way to make an institutional film cheap.

Provide an **instrumental-only stem** and a **VO-only stem** in addition to the mix, so the mix can be rebuilt if the narration read changes.

---

## Sound design ownership

The score does **not** own these. They are sound design, from `MASTER-SCRIPT.md` §10:

- The notification timbres across Acts 1–3 (thin two-tone ping, low buzz, etc.) — these are the sound designer's, and they must be recognisable in Act 5 when the system resolves them
- The logo resolve tone at S26 and S60
- Room tone for the eight-second silence
- All paper, keyboard, phone and campus ambience

The one exception is S47's notification tone, listed under M7 above. Keep it musically integrated — it is the one sound that belongs to both tracks.

## Delivery

| Item | Spec |
|---|---|
| Full mix | 7:00, 48kHz/24-bit WAV, −6 dBFS peak |
| Stems | piano, synth, percussion, sub, strings — each 7:00 |
| VO-only stem | For re-mix against a new read |
| Music-only stem | For re-mix against a new VO |
| Alternate | 15s and 30s versions, for festival and social cutdowns |
| Silence check | Confirm 1:52–2:00 is digital silence in the delivered mix |

**If the score is generated rather than composed:** it must still be delivered as stems, and it must be conformed to the 76 BPM grid before delivery. A generated cue that drifts off tempo by S27 will not sit under a diagram with eight timed ignitions.
